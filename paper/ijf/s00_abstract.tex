\begin{abstract}
\noindent We evaluate daily wholesale price forecasts for 11,845 Indian mandi series (36 commodities) at four horizons.
The evaluation uses target-purged chronological splits, strong baselines, a provenance audit and post-development scoring.
Persistence is hard to beat, because a fifth to a third of price changes are exactly zero.
Among global learners only absolute-loss models beat it at the next report, and gains reach 3--8\%.
A public mirror holds only 30\% of official reports from October 2025, around a change of publishing portal.
On the mirror's own rows, interval coverage drops to 68--72\%; on the complete record the same rows give 79--80\%.
Frozen models still beat persistence on six later months of official reports.
Retraining the Spark models on two further years gave no measurable gain.
A global LightGBM beat the Spark models and, under a pre-registered rule, replaced them in service.
Provenance audits should precede claims of drift.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} agricultural prices; forecast evaluation; data provenance; prospective evaluation; conformal prediction; benchmarks
