\section{Targets, chronological design and metrics}\label{sec:protocol}
\paragraph{Targets.} For entity $e$, origin date $t$ and modal price $P_{e,t}$, predict the log return to the first report in $[t+h,t+h+3]$ for $h=3,7,14$ calendar days. The next-report target is the first subsequent report within seven days. If no report exists, that origin/horizon is unlabelled, not imputed. The numerical forecast is $P_{e,t}\exp(\hat y)$. All price information used for features is dated at or before the origin; same-day cross-market reporting availability is an explicit assumption, not established intra-day availability.

\paragraph{Periods and budget.} Training origins and targets lie in 2024; 500,000 deterministic hash-selected rows per horizon train each variant. January--June 2025 selects leaf count and early-stopping iteration; July--December 2025 calibrates interval residuals and volatility bins. Retrospective evaluation runs from 2026-01-01 through 2026-10-22. Target dates are purged at each period end. Category encoding and training scales use training rows only. Both variants use seed 20261023, leaf counts 31/63, at most 800 boosting iterations and early stopping after 75 rounds. Selection observations are not reused for calibration. However, all 2026 outcomes were already available during study design, so this separation is not independent confirmation or a pristine future test.

\paragraph{Different error questions.} MAE is the mean absolute rupee error across eligible evaluation rows, and relative MAE divides candidate MAE by contemporaneous persistence MAE on identical rows. Frequently reported and high-error series can dominate this pooled measure. For entity $e$ and horizon $h$, MASE divides each absolute error by that entity's mean absolute naive $h$-target error over all eligible \emph{training} rows, and then averages over rows with positive finite scales \cite{hyndman2006another}. It is not division by evaluation-period persistence MAE and is not equal weighting of markets. Rows without a usable training scale are excluded only from MASE (4.4--4.5\% here), not from full-row MAE. Therefore a relative MAE below one can coexist with MASE near or above one. Neither establishes universal superiority.
We also retain RMSE, WAPE, bias, subgroup errors, entity-ratio quantiles, interval coverage and width in the artifacts. Coverage of a nominal price interval is the observed fraction of realised prices inside it, distinct from uncertainty about an aggregate MAE ratio.

\paragraph{Dependence sensitivity.} Temporal analysis aggregates all same-day cross-market losses and counts. There are 281--294 calendar days, not half a million independent replicates. We retain all circular/non-circular moving-block specifications of 28, 42 and 56 days, with 2,000 replicates each; endpoints are approximate percentile sensitivity estimates, not validated coverage guarantees. A 56-day span gives only about five non-overlapping spans, not additional independent seasons. Block choice and dependence assumptions matter \cite{politis2004block}. Secondary asymptotic normal HAC diagnostics use bandwidths 28/42/56 without a fixed-horizon HLN correction. Each bandwidth has its own Holm-adjusted eight-comparison family; no favourable specification is selected. These choices followed inspection of earlier results and are explicitly retrospective. Original heuristic p values are retained only as legacy diagnostics in artifacts.

Daily loss/count and autocorrelation diagnostics, commodity/state summaries and exploratory entity resampling describe sensitivity. Entities sharing markets, regions and commodity shocks are not independent markets. None of these summaries establishes stationarity, two-way inferential coverage, training uncertainty or future transportability. A human statistical review remains outstanding.
