Short lessons on reading prices and the outlook.

## Modal, lowest and highest price
Each market report gives three prices for one quintal (100 kg).

The lowest and highest are the smallest and biggest prices paid that day. The modal price is the price paid most often. Use it as the normal price.

Example: tomato in Kolar. Lowest ₹800, highest ₹1,400, modal ₹1,100. Most farmers got about ₹1,100 per quintal.

## Price trends
A trend shows how the price moved in the last 30 days: rising, falling or steady.

Price swings show how much the price jumps from day to day. Big swings mean more risk.

A trend is about the past. It does not promise that the price will keep moving the same way.

## Reading a forecast
A forecast gives a middle estimate and a range for the next report, and for 3, 7 and 14 days ahead.

The middle estimate is the single most likely number. The range shows where the price will probably be.

Example: onion in Lasalgaon, last price ₹1,500. In 7 days: middle estimate ₹1,580, range ₹1,350 to ₹1,800.

## Why forecasts can be wrong
Rain, festivals, transport strikes, government decisions and large arrivals from other states can change prices suddenly.

The model learns only from past market reports. It cannot know about these events.

The app shows how wrong the forecast was in past tests, so you know how much to trust it.

## Uncertainty ranges
In past tests, the 80% range held the real price about 8 times out of 10. The 90% range is wider and held it about 9 times out of 10.

So sometimes the real price falls outside the range.

A wide range means the forecast is unsure. Be careful with big decisions.

## Comparing markets
The same crop can get different prices in nearby markets on the same day.

Compare only recent reports. A price from a week ago is not a fair comparison, so it is marked “older report”.

Example: tomato ₹1,100 in Kolar and ₹1,250 in Chintamani. Subtract your transport cost before you decide.

## Transport and storage costs
Moving produce costs money: vehicle, loading, commission and market fees.

Storing produce also costs money. Tomato, leafy vegetables and fruit lose quality fast. Grains and pulses keep longer.

Enter your own costs in the outlook card. The outlook is worked out again with your numbers.

## Using SELL, HOLD, WATCH and COMPARE
SELL: even the higher estimate is not above the last price. Selling now may be worth considering.

HOLD: even the lower estimate is above the last price plus storage costs. WATCH: there is no clear signal.

COMPARE: a nearby market recently reported a clearly higher price. Check it before you travel.

## No guarantee of future price
These signals are a market outlook. They are not a promise and not financial advice.

The app does not know your costs, your crop quality or your need for cash.

Use the outlook as one input. Also talk to traders, your FPO or the agriculture extension officer.

## When data is missing or old
Some markets do not report every day. Then the latest price can be several days old.

Always look at the report date. If it is more than 3 days old, the app shows a warning.

If your market has no recent report, look at nearby markets or call the market office (APMC).
