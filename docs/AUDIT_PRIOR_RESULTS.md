## Methodological problems in the previous anomaly classifier

1. **The label is a deterministic function of the features (circularity).** If the "Task 2" target is `|z_score| > 2.0` on the *current* row, with `z_score = (modal − rolling_avg)/rolling_std`, then `modal_price`, `rolling_7_observation_avg` and `rolling_7_observation_stddev` are themselves inputs. The model only has to rebuild a formula, so high accuracy says nothing about anomaly detection.
2. **Leakage in `price_outlier`.** `AnomalyPrediction.scala` uses `lead(price_outlier)` as its label. `price_outlier` comes from `OutlierTreatment.learnBounds`, whose per-commodity IQR bounds are fitted on **all years, including the 2025 test period**.
3. **Accuracy on imbalanced data.** With about 15% positives, accuracy is dominated by the majority class. The logs confirm the all-normal baseline (84.35%) beats the reported model (72.54%).
4. **No forecasting target.** The project title promises price forecasting, but no model predicted a price.
5. **Tiny sample.** Entity-level hash sampling kept about 0.1% of entities (`% 1000 === 0`) to avoid out-of-memory errors.

## What replaced it

* A price-forecasting benchmark (`forecast/*.scala`) with:
  * chronological train, validation and test splits that purge on target dates;
  * persistence, moving-average and seasonal baselines;
  * MASE, WAPE and Diebold–Mariano tests;
  * perturbation tests that fail if any feature reads the future (`ForecastFeaturesSpec`).
* An anomaly rule scored only against trailing statistics, evaluated with injected, labelled corruptions against simple baselines (`py/agri/analysis/anomaly_benchmark.py`). It makes no claim about accuracy on unlabelled real anomalies.
* All reported numbers are generated from the files in `results/forecast/<experiment>/` and `results/anomaly/<run>/` (see `docs/EXPERIMENTS.md`).

The legacy classes under `src/main/scala/com/agribigdata/ml/` are kept for history and still compile. They are no longer part of the pipeline.
